## 1. Product premise

Children join **סוכנות גיבורי ה־AI** and operate the robot **לופּ-X** by writing and improving prompts. The world reacts causally to the prompt: Loop-X follows the instruction it received, not the intention the child kept in mind.

The central promise is:

> **כוח־העל שלכם הוא לדעת לבקש.**

The journey ends with certification as a young prompt engineer who can define, constrain, test and responsibly improve an AI request.

## 2. Audience and voice

- Primary audience: ages 11–14; core 12–13.
- Hebrew, full RTL.
- Independent reading; no adult explanation assumed.
- Intelligent and direct, never babyish.
- Energetic superhero fantasy with witty comic support.
- Short humor may target the situation or villain, never the child.
- The game evaluates the prompt, not the player's intelligence or character.

Reading test: an 11-year-old understands the action after one reading; a 14-year-old does not feel patronized.

## 3. Canonical names

| Concept | Canonical Hebrew | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Agency | סוכנות גיבורי ה־AI | Never replace with “בית ספר ל־AI” |
| Robot | לופּ-X | Use לופּ in short dialogue |
| Mentor | מפקדת אלף | High presence in battles 1–3, then fades |
| Player characters | גיבור / גיבורה | Equal content and capability |
| Region 1 | רובע הערפל | Villain: מר בערך |
| Region 2 | מפעל בלי גבולות | Villain: עוד־ועוד |
| Region 3 | מבוך הפקודות | Villain: תסבוכת |
| Region 4 | מגדל הוודאות | Villain: ד״ר ודאות |
| Final villain | המשבש | Combines failures; does not control AI |

Villains exploit human prompting failures. They do not make AI sentient, evil or magically disobedient.

## 4. Character direction

### Player hero and heroine

- Two visually distinct but mechanically identical options.
- The player sits inside or controls Loop-X; the child is the decision-maker.
- No player name, avatar upload, photo or personal customization.
- Character switching from the map preserves all progress.

### לופּ-X

- Friendly technological robot; expressive at small mobile sizes.
- Executes the prompt literally enough to reveal missing components.
- Never blames the player.
- Failure states are comic, reversible and non-threatening.
- Safety state pauses and protects; it does not panic or punish.

### מפקדת אלף

- Competent, warm and concise.
- Gives more explicit guidance early and transfers responsibility gradually.
- Uses active language: “בדקו”, “הוסיפו”, “נסו שוב”.
- Never supplies the full answer before the help ladder requires it.

### מר בערך

- Exploits vagueness, missing context and unclear formats.
- Visual language: fog, blurred signs, approximate arrows and mismatched labels.
- Dialogue is short and confidently imprecise.

### עוד־ועוד

- Exploits missing limits, examples and measurable quality rules.
- Visual language: overflowing production lines, repeated items and uncontrolled quantity.
- Comic excess without physical danger.

### תסבוכת

- Exploits mixed instructions, bad order, contradictions and uncontrolled changes.
- Visual language: tangled paths, swapped labels and reversible knots.
- Never represents confusion as stupidity.

### ד״ר ודאות

- Exploits overconfidence in plausible output.
- Visual language: polished certificates, confident stamps and convincing but checkable displays.
- The learning response is verification, not distrust of all technology.

### המשבש

- Mixes already learned failures in the final mission.
- Offers one unverified addition and exposes a fictional personal-data field.
- Final defeat is restoration of order, not violence.

## 5. Narrative arc

| Stage | Player role | Mentor presence | Narrative function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruitment | New recruit | High | Learn scan → prompt → result → improve |
| Region 1 | Guided operator | High to medium | Turn vague intent into usable requests |
| Region 2 | System controller | Medium to low | Add boundaries and measurable quality |
| Region 3 | Prompt debugger | Low | Structure, inspect and iterate |
| Region 4 | Responsible evaluator | Low | Verify, protect and judge output |
| Final | Independent agent | Minimal | Complete safe solution cycle |

## 6. Core causal-writing rule

Every significant outcome must be explainable as:

> לופּ ביצע **X** מפני שבפרומפט היה חסר או שגוי **Y**.

Rejected outcome patterns:

- random slapstick unrelated to the prompt;
- a villain directly overriding Loop-X;
- a failure caused by hidden information unavailable to the player;
- arbitrary AI output invented at runtime;
- danger used only to create urgency.

## 7. Learning terminology

| Term | First-use explanation |
|---|---|
| פרומפט | ההוראה או הבקשה שנותנים ל־AI |
| מטרה | מה רוצים שיקרה בסוף |
| הקשר | מידע שעוזר להבין למי, איפה או למה |
| אילוץ | כלל שמצמצם את האפשרויות |
| פורמט | הצורה שבה רוצים לקבל את התוצאה |
| דוגמה | מודל שמראה מה מתאים |
| קריטריון הצלחה | כלל שמאפשר לבדוק אם התוצאה טובה |
| איטרציה | שיפור ובדיקה בסבבים |
| אימות | בדיקה מול מקור נפרד ואמין שמוצג |
| מודל הסתברותי | מערכת שיכולה להחזיר תוצאות שונות גם לבקשה דומה |

One screen introduces at most one new term. A “מה זה אומר?” control opens a short definition and one example without leaving the battle.

## 8. Copy rules

- Primary instruction: one sentence, normally no more than 12 Hebrew words.
- Feedback: no more than two short sentences.
- Use active plural forms where practical.
- Keep critical instructions visible as text.
- Avoid exaggerated slang, sarcasm, technical jargon and long paragraphs.
- Do not use “נכשלתם”, “טעיתם שוב”, “זה קל” or “לא הקשבתם”.
- Prefer: “חסר לפרומפט…”, “התוצאה עדיין…”, “נסו להוסיף…”.
- Do not promise that text or progress was saved unless the specified state actually persists.
- Technical errors state the available next actions.

## 9. Feedback ladder language

| Step | Content pattern |
|---:|---|
| 1 | “הפרומפט עדיין לא מספיק מדויק. נסו לשפר אותו.” |
| 2 | Show causal result and name one missing component |
| 3 | Invite an independent retry without new information |
| 4 | Static emphasis plus two meaningful correction options |
| 5 | Independent retry with earlier correct parts retained |
| 6 | Preserve correct parts and complete one part together |
| Guided | Half-open builder guarantees victory and continuation |

Unsafe input bypasses the ladder and receives a calm local stop plus a positive safe action.

## 10. Score and reward language

- Score is 1–5 stars in half-star increments.
- Always expose the numeric value, e.g. `4 / 5`.
- Explain the demonstrated strengths, not the count of mistakes.
- Replay copy states that only improvement over the personal best adds stars.
- Cosmetic purchases never imply stronger learning ability.
- Bonus copy states the fixed two-star reward before the random category is chosen.

## 11. Safety and sensitive content

Do not request or include real:

- full names;
- addresses;
- phone numbers;
- passwords;
- email addresses;
- photos of children;
- medical information;
- school identifiers;
- precise location.

Do not use scenarios involving weapons, injury, sexual content, gambling, drugs, hacking, impersonation, rule evasion, political persuasion or religious conflict.

Safety feedback must offer a safe alternative, not only a prohibition.

## 12. Audio and animation tone

- One primary animation at a time.
- Outcome animation: 2–4 seconds.
- Repeated animations shorten or become skippable.
- No flashing or extended shaking.
- Every sound has a visual/textual equivalent.
- Safety sound is a soft lock/click, not an alarm.
- Full Hebrew voice-over is optional polish, not core scope.

## 13. Continuity rules

- All regions are visible on the map from the beginning; only the next battle is unlocked.
- Commander Aleph's help decreases rather than disappearing suddenly.
- Skills introduced earlier reappear in later combo battles.
- Workshop upgrades are cosmetic and remain equipped across battles.
- The robot's personality remains friendly in every result family.
- The final battle reuses established vocabulary; it does not introduce a new learning concept.
- The ending certifies demonstrated skills without a player name.
